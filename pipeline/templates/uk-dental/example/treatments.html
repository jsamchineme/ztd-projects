Treatments we offer

Most of these are available on the NHS. Some, like tooth whitening, are private only. Ask us if you’re not sure.

Check-ups and everyday care

Check-ups
Your dentist checks your teeth, gums and mouth. NHS check-ups are free for everyone in Scotland.
Gum treatment (periodontal treatment)
Cleaning off hardened plaque (tartar), and showing you how to brush and floss well.
White and amalgam fillings
Tooth-coloured or silver-coloured fillings to repair a hole in a tooth.
Taking out a tooth (extraction)
When a tooth can’t be saved.

Repairing and replacing teeth

Root canal treatment
Clears infection from inside a tooth so you can keep it.
Crowns
A cap that covers and protects a weak or broken tooth.
Inlays and onlays
Made-to-fit fillings for bigger holes, made outside the mouth and fixed in place.
Bridges
A false tooth fixed to the teeth on either side of a gap.
Dentures and repairs
Removable false teeth, and repairs to dentures you already have.

What NHS treatment costs

The Scottish Government sets NHS dental charges. They are the same at every NHS dentist in Scotland.

Free

Check-ups for everyone

NHS check-ups are free for everyone in Scotland, whatever your age.

Free

NHS treatment if you are:

  • under 26
  • pregnant, or had a baby in the last 12 months
  • on certain benefits or a low income, for example with an HC2 certificate

80%

Everyone else pays 80% of the cost

The most you pay for one course of NHS treatment is £384. An HC3 certificate pays for part of the cost.

Help with costs

If you find NHS charges hard to pay

  • An HC2 certificate means your NHS treatment is free. An HC3 certificate pays for part of it.
  • To apply, fill in an HC1 form for the NHS Low Income Scheme. Ask us for one at reception.
  • Bring proof that you get free treatment to every appointment.

Paying

How to pay for your treatment

  • NHS patients who pay charges pay as their treatment goes along.
  • Private treatment is paid for on the day.
  • We take cash, cheques and debit or credit cards.
  • For a big course of treatment, ask our practice manager, Jackie, about ways to pay.

Check-ups

How often will I have a check-up?

Your dentist decides how often you need a check-up, based on the health of your mouth. It can be anything from every few months to every 2 years. Being seen less often is a sign that your mouth is healthy.

You don’t have to wait for your check-up. Phone us if you notice any change in your mouth, especially a lump, bump or ulcer that hasn’t gone away after 3 weeks.

Will I still get a scale and polish?

If you need one, yes. After your check-up, your dentist may decide that advice on brushing and looking after your gums will help you more. A scale and polish is still there if you need it.

Fillings and treatment

Can I have white fillings on the NHS?

On the NHS, front teeth usually get white fillings and back teeth get amalgam (silver-coloured) fillings. You can have white fillings on back teeth on the NHS if:

  • you are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • you had a baby in the last 12 months
  • you are under 15
  • you have a letter from a hospital specialist saying you need them
  • your dentist thinks you need them for a health reason

This doesn’t mean old amalgam fillings will be replaced. You can choose a private white filling on a tooth that is otherwise treated on the NHS.

When can I eat or drink after a filling or a tooth out?

White fillings are usually set before you leave. Amalgam fillings take 24 hours to set fully.

Your mouth may stay numb for 1 to 3 hours. Until it wears off, take care not to burn yourself with hot food or drink, or bite your lip or cheek. Ask your dentist before you leave if you’re not sure.

How long does a referral to the dental hospital take?

It depends on why you are referred.

  • Routine referrals, for example to take out a tooth or to treat a child, can take up to 12 weeks for a first appointment.
  • Urgent referrals, for example pain with a fever, or a problem spreading to your face, are seen sooner.
  • If mouth cancer is suspected, the hospital sees you as soon as possible.

Paying

Can I have private treatment and stay an NHS patient?

Yes. You pay private prices for the private treatment, and you keep all your NHS care. Your dentist will talk you through the options and costs first.

What if I can’t afford NHS charges?

You may get help through the NHS Low Income Scheme. It looks at your income to work out how much, if anything, you pay. Ask us for an HC1 form at reception.

What happens if I miss an appointment?

Please phone us at least 24 hours before to cancel. If you don’t, we may charge you.

Getting here

Can I be seen if I can’t use stairs?

Yes. If you can’t go upstairs, or you use a wheelchair, you can be seen in surgery 1. Tell reception or your dentist when you book.

Where can I park?

There are free parking spaces in front of the practice, and free parking along Musselburgh High Street. There is also a free car park at Kerr’s Wynd.

Can I have an interpreter?

Yes. NHS patients can have an interpreter for free. Phone the NHS Lothian Interpretation Service on 0131 242 8181. Dr Martin Chan also speaks Cantonese.
