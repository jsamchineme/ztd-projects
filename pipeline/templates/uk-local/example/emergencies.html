Toothache and dental emergencies

If you are registered with us, phone us first. This page tells you who to call, day and night.

Call 01955 609949

Open Monday to Friday from 8am

How urgent is it?

Find the card that matches your problem. The red cards are the most urgent.

Immediate action required: Call 999 or go to A&E if you have:

  • swelling in your mouth, throat or neck that makes it hard to breathe, speak or swallow
  • heavy bleeding from your mouth that will not stop
  • a serious injury to your face or jaw

Urgent advice: Phone us, or 111 when we are closed, if you have:

  • an abscess or swelling in or around your mouth
  • bleeding
  • a knocked-out adult tooth. If you can, put it back in and come to us as soon as possible

We are open: 01955 609949

We are closed: NHS 24 on 111

Non-urgent advice: Phone us on the next working day if:

  • you have broken or chipped a tooth. Keep the broken piece in milk
  • your dentures are broken
  • a crown or bridge has come out
  • a filling has come out and you have no pain

Not registered with a dentist? For these problems, phone the NHS Highland Dental Helpline on 0800 141 2362 on a weekday.

Who to call

Who you call depends on whether you are registered with us, and the time of day.

If you are registered with us

When we are open: 01955 609949

When we are closed: NHS 24 on 111

When you phone, we will tell you if you should see the dentist or go to a hospital.

If you are not registered with a dentist

Monday to Friday, 8:45am to 5pm: 0800 141 2362

This is the NHS Highland Dental Helpline. It is free to call.

At other times: NHS 24 on 111

Common problems and what to do

Open a question to see what to do while you wait to see a dentist.

Pain, swelling and bleeding

I have toothache

Phone us as soon as we open. When we are closed, phone NHS 24 on 111.

If you take pain relief, always stick to the dose on the label or leaflet.

How much pain relief can I take?

Always stick to the recommended dose on the label or leaflet.

If you think you have taken more than the most you should, phone 111 straight away.

My face or mouth is swollen

Swelling in or around your mouth is a dental emergency. Phone us, or 111 when we are closed.

If the swelling makes it hard to breathe, speak or swallow, call 999 or go to A&E.

My mouth is bleeding

Bleeding is a dental emergency. Phone us, or 111 when we are closed.

If the bleeding is heavy and will not stop, call 999 or go to A&E.

Teeth, fillings and dentures

I have knocked out a tooth

If it is an adult tooth, find it and try to put it back in. Then phone us, or 111 when we are closed. You need to see a dentist as soon as possible.

Do not put a baby tooth back in. Take your child to see a dentist as soon as possible.

I have broken or chipped a tooth

This is not an emergency. If you can find the broken piece, keep it in milk.

Phone us on the next working day.

A crown or bridge has come out

This is not an emergency. Phone us on the next working day.

You can buy an emergency dental kit from a supermarket or pharmacy.

A filling has come out

If you have no pain, this is not an emergency. Phone us on the next working day.

If you are in pain, phone us as soon as we open.

My dentures are broken

This is not an emergency. Phone us on the next working day.
