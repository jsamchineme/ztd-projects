Dental pain and emergencies

If you are registered with us, phone us first. Here is who to call and when.

Immediate action required: Call 999 or go to A&E if you have:

  • swelling in your mouth, throat or neck that makes it hard to breathe, speak or swallow
  • heavy bleeding from your mouth that will not stop
  • a serious injury to your face or jaw

Urgent advice: Phone us, or 111 when we are closed, if you have:

  • an abscess or swelling in or around your mouth
  • bleeding
  • a knocked-out adult tooth. If you can, put it back in and come to us as soon as possible

We are open: 01955 609949

We are closed: NHS 24 on 111

Non-urgent advice: Phone us on the next working day if:

  • you have broken or chipped a tooth. Keep the broken piece in milk
  • your dentures are broken
  • a crown or bridge has come out
  • a filling has come out and you have no pain

Not registered with a dentist? On weekdays, phone the NHS Highland Dental Helpline on 0800 141 2362 (8:45am to 5pm). At other times, phone 111.

Joining the practice

We cannot always take new NHS patients. Phone us to ask before you visit.

  1. Phone us

    Call 01955 609949 and ask if we can take you as a new patient.

  2. Give us your details

    We will ask for your name, address, date of birth and contact details.

  3. Come for your first check-up

    This is a full examination. You might be on a waiting list until then. If you need help sooner, tell us when you phone.

Feeling nervous? Tell us when you phone. We know visiting a dentist can be a matter of nerves, and we aim to make your visit as calm as we can.

Read the guide for new patients

Good to know

  • You do not pay a deposit to register.
  • Register your children when you register yourself. You can register a baby as soon as they are born.
  • Once registered, you stay registered unless you or the dentist ask to end it.
  • If English is not your first language, you can ask for an interpreter. It is free for NHS patients.

What NHS treatment costs

These are the NHS rules in Scotland. Ask us what your treatment will cost before it starts.

Free

NHS check-ups, for everyone in Scotland.

NHS treatment is also free if you are:

  • under 26
  • pregnant, or you had a baby in the last 12 months
  • getting certain benefits, or you have an HC2 certificate for low income

Bring proof with you, and tell us before your treatment starts.

Everyone else

80%

You pay 80% of the cost of NHS treatment.

£384

The most you will pay for one course of NHS treatment.

An HC3 certificate from the NHS Low Income Scheme can pay part of the cost.

Private treatment: ask us for a quote. General Dental Council standards say dentists must give you a written treatment plan with the cost, and say if it is NHS or private.
