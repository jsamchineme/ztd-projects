Joining as a new patient

We cannot always take new NHS patients. Phone us to ask before you visit.

Call 01955 609949

How to join

Joining takes four steps. You do not pay a deposit to register.

Are we taking new patients? Our last notice said we had stopped taking new patients for now. This may have changed, so phone to check.

  1. Phone us

    Call 01955 609949 and ask if we can take you as a new NHS patient.

  2. Give us your details

    We will ask for your name, address, date of birth and contact details.

  3. Fill in a medical history form

    You do this before your first check-up. Have a list of all the medicines you take.

  4. Come for your first check-up

    This is a full examination. You might be on a waiting list until then. If you need help sooner, tell us when you phone.

Feeling nervous? Tell us when you phone. We know visiting a dentist can be a matter of nerves, and we aim to make your visit as calm as we can.

Good to know

  • Register your children when you register yourself. You can register a baby as soon as they are born.
  • Once registered, you stay registered unless you or the dentist ask to end it.
  • If English is not your first language, you can ask for an interpreter. It is free for NHS patients.

Your first visit, step by step

Your first check-up is longer than usual. Here is what happens.

  1. We ask about your health

    We check your medical history, your medicines and any allergies. We may ask if you smoke or drink alcohol.

  2. We examine your mouth

    The dentist checks your teeth, gums and tongue. Tell us about any lumps, ulcers or problems you have noticed.

  3. We explain what you need

    If you need treatment, we explain your options and what they cost. We tell you which are NHS and which are private.

  4. You get a treatment plan

    It says what treatment you need and roughly what it will cost. You can ask for a copy to take home.

  5. We tell you when to come back

    This depends on your needs. It can be from less than 6 months to every 2 years.

What to bring

  • A list of all the medicines you take Your medical history is updated every year.
  • Proof if you get free NHS treatment For example, a maternity exemption certificate.
  • Your children’s details If you want to register them too.

What NHS treatment costs

These are the NHS rules in Scotland. Ask us what your treatment will cost before it starts.

Free

NHS check-ups, for everyone in Scotland.

NHS treatment is also free if you are:

  • under 26
  • pregnant, or you had a baby in the last 12 months
  • exempt because of your income or certain benefits, and you have proof

Bring proof with you, and tell us before your treatment starts.

Everyone else

80%

You pay 80% of the cost of NHS treatment.

£384

The most you will pay for one course of NHS treatment.

Getting help with costs

I am pregnant or had a baby in the last 12 months

Your NHS dental treatment is free. You need an NHS Maternity Exemption Certificate.

Get the form from your GP practice, midwife or health visitor. They will help you fill it in and send it. Your certificate comes by post.

I get benefits

Your NHS dental treatment is free if you are included in an award of:

  • Income Support
  • income-related Employment and Support Allowance
  • income-based Jobseeker’s Allowance
  • Pension Credit Guarantee Credit
  • some Universal Credit awards, depending on your earnings

Tell us which benefit you get, and bring proof.

I am on a low income

The NHS Low Income Scheme may pay some or all of your NHS charges. To apply, fill in form HC1.

You can get the form from a pharmacy, GP practice, Citizens Advice Scotland office or Jobcentre Plus.

For help with the form: 0300 330 1343

Can I be charged for a missed appointment?

NHS rules allow a dentist to charge you if you miss an appointment without telling them in time. A dentist can also ask you to pay in advance.

If you cannot come, phone us as soon as you can.
