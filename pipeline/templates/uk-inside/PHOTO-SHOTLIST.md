# Photo shot list: "See inside before you visit"

**For:** the practice manager, or a local photographer the practice books. **Time:** about 2 hours, before opening or after closing. **Gear:** a recent phone is fine; a camera with a 24 to 35mm lens is better. No flash.

**Why these photos:** many patients are nervous, and not knowing what to expect makes it worse. These photos let someone see the building, the door, the desk, the chair and the faces before they come. Each one has a job. If a shot doesn't help a patient find the place or know what to expect, skip it.

## The patient's journey: 12 shots, in order

| # | Shot | Framing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The building from where people arrive** | Landscape. Stand where a patient first sees it (the car park, the bus stop or across the street). Include the door and any landmark next to it. | The most important photo. Shoot on a bright, overcast day so the front isn't in hard shadow. |
| 2 | **The sign and the door** | Landscape, closer. The sign and the entrance in one frame, straight on. | Doors open or closed, but clean glass. Show the handle or button. |
| 3 | **Step-free access** (if you have it) | The ramp, level threshold or automatic door, and the disabled parking bay. | Only if it's true. Patients plan around this. |
| 4 | **Parking or the bus stop** | Wide, showing the spaces nearest the door. | Optional if shot 1 already shows them. |
| 5 | **Coming in: the first view inside** | Landscape, from just inside the door, at eye level (about 1.6m). | This is what a nervous patient sees first. |
| 6 | **The reception desk** | Landscape, slightly to one side, so the desk and the screen or sign above it read clearly. | A receptionist behind the desk, smiling, is good (with consent). No screens showing names. |
| 7 | **The waiting area** | Wide, from a corner. Show the seats, toys or books, baby changing if you have it. | Empty of patients. Tidy the magazines. |
| 8 | **The corridor or stairs to the surgeries** | Portrait or landscape, looking along the route. | Shows how far it is and whether there are stairs. |
| 9 | **A treatment room, wide** | Landscape, from the doorway, the chair upright, the light off. | The single most reassuring room photo. Clear the worktops. Blinds half open for daylight. |
| 10 | **The chair, from the patient's side** | Portrait, sitting height, looking at the chair and the dentist's stool. | A staff member can sit in the chair. |
| 11 | **Something patients notice** | The x-ray screen, the sterilising room, the hygienist's room or a quiet room. | Pick what your patients ask about. No patient images or names on screen. |
| 12 | **The people** | One headshot per dentist, hygienist, nurse, receptionist and manager. Head and shoulders, plain light wall, window light from the side, same height and crop for everyone. Portrait 4:5 or 5:6. | Uniform as worn. A natural smile. Shoot everyone on the same day so they match. Add a group photo of the whole team if you can. |

**Optional extras:** a 60 to 90 second phone video walking from the door to the chair (steady, no music needed), and a photo of any sensory or quiet room.

## Light and framing

- Daylight where possible. Turn on all room lights. Avoid mixing very warm and very cold light.
- Keep verticals straight: hold the phone level, don't tilt up at buildings from close by. Step back instead.
- Shoot landscape (3:2 or 4:3) unless the table says portrait. Leave a little space around the subject; the website will crop.
- No filters, no heavy editing, no "beauty" retouching. The point is that it looks the same when they arrive.
- Remove clutter: paperwork, bins, personal bags, cleaning products, cables.

## Privacy and consent

- **No patients** in any photo unless the practice has their written consent for website use. Shoot when the practice is closed.
- **No personal data in shot:** appointment screens, day lists, x-rays with names, post, noticeboards with names, car number plates you can read (blur them or reframe).
- **Staff:** each person signs a consent form before their photo is used. Keep the forms. Anyone can withdraw consent, and their photo comes off the website.
- **Children:** only with a parent's or guardian's written consent, never named.
- If a photo includes someone who later leaves, replace or remove it.

### Consent form (one per person; copy onto practice letterhead)

> I agree that [Practice name] may use photographs of me, taken on [date], on its website and printed patient information. I understand they will be shown with my first name and job title only. I can withdraw this consent at any time by telling the practice manager, and the photos will be removed from the website within 14 days.
>
> Name: ____________ Role: ____________ Signature: ____________ Date: ________
>
> For a child (under 16): Parent or guardian name and signature: ____________

## Files to send

- Original files, full size, JPEG or HEIC, straight from the camera or phone. Don't send screenshots or WhatsApp copies (they're compressed).
- Name them by shot number: `01-building.jpg`, `06-reception.jpg`, `12-headshot-anne-wyllie.jpg`.
- For each person, write their name and job title exactly as they want it shown.
- Send by a file-sharing link (for example OneDrive or Google Drive), not email attachments.
- We'll resize everything for the website (each photo ends up around 30 to 90 KB) and remove location data.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] Shots 1, 2, 5, 6, 9 and 12 done (the essentials).
- [ ] No patients, no names on screens, no readable number plates.
- [ ] A signed consent form for every person pictured.
- [ ] Each headshot has a name and job title written down.
- [ ] Originals sent by a sharing link.
