# Custom design patterns on private dental websites (Scotland)

Studied on 12 October 2026. The sample is 131 independent dental practices graded "Good" in `prospects/` whose sites signal private work (private, cosmetic, implants, Invisalign, aesthetics). Excluded: NHS board clinics (PDS), corporate groups (Clyde Munro, Portman, mydentist and others) and duplicate sites. Every homepage was reviewed. Full pages were captured for the 10 most distinctive sites.

## Headline finding

Most "good" private sites are not custom. About 4 in 5 are the same few agency layouts:
- a dark full-bleed photo with a centred serif headline and two buttons
- a cookie wall, an AI chat widget (ReceptionPal, Dentr) and often a lead-form pop-up on arrival

One agency layout ("Refer a patient · Call · Book online", tan buttons) appears unchanged on Care Dental Crieff, Bothwell, Aviemore, Westhill and Oak Tree. Truly custom sites are rare. That gap is the opportunity.

## Patterns, from most to least custom

1. **Editorial typography with an italic accent word.** For example "Right here in *Glenrothes*" or "Care that feels considered, *not clinical*". Often uses giant ghosted words behind sections and a marquee ribbon ("NOW ACCEPTING NHS & PRIVATE PATIENTS").
2. **The room as the brand.** Interior photography of real rooms (window views, fireplaces, staircases, timber reception desks) instead of stock smiling faces. Images use arched or rounded masks and warm tones.
3. **Proof of results.** Before/after comparison sliders, smile galleries, and treatment cards showing the result.
4. **Transparent "From £" pricing** on treatment cards and a price list in the main menu.
5. **Treatment index with a detail panel.** A list of treatments on the left and the selected one's detail on the right, or a photo mosaic of treatments.
6. **Patient stories as content.** A testimonial as the hero headline, patient video stories, and Google review cards with names.
7. **Outcome and trust stats.** Years established, patients treated, implants placed, success rate, same-day emergencies.
8. **A dedicated nervous-patient section** with an empathetic headline, such as "For people who would rather be anywhere else".
9. **Principles and journey.** Numbered "Our approach" principles, or a 4-step "smile journey" (consultation, plan, treatment, aftercare).
10. **Founder and "the why".** A short video or letter from the principal dentist.
11. **Smile tools.** An AI smile analyser, upload-a-photo simulation, a virtual consultation, a treatment chooser.
12. **Finance and plans up front.** Monthly plans ("from £16.27/month") and 0% finance as a hero band.
13. **Awards and press.** "As featured on" logo bands, award badges, BDA Good Practice, supplier logos.
14. **Social and referrals.** An Instagram feed, a WhatsApp button, and a referral pathway for other dentists.

## What to avoid (common on these sites, and against the NHS research we follow)

- **Pop-ups that block the page on arrival:** cookie walls, lead forms ("Invisalign open day"), offer modals ("Special offer £99"). Overlays stopped 27% of research participants.
- **AI chat widgets** that cover the page and the call button on phones.
- **Auto-rotating carousels and video backgrounds.**
- **Light grey text on cream, and thin serif body text.** Poor contrast.
- **"Book online" with no phone number visible on phones.**
- **Stock models presented as patients.**

## Implication for our templates

A private-practice template can use patterns 1–10 and 12–13 while keeping our rules: tasks first on a phone, no pop-ups, real photos only, WCAG 2.2 AA. Real photos only means before/after photos must have patient consent, and interiors must be the practice's own. Pattern 11 (smile tools) needs a real backend, so leave it as a link to a consultation instead.
