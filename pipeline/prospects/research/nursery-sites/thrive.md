# Thrive Childcare and Education

Studied 13 October 2026 by crawl (40 pages) and screenshots at 390px and 1280px (home, book a tour, fees, one nursery page, curriculum). Scratchpad folder: `study-3/thrive/`.

**Note on scope:** Thrive is an English-headquartered group (Media City, Salford) with 46 nurseries across England and Scotland (Cheshire, Greater Manchester, Liverpool and Yorkshire, plus Glasgow and the west, Edinburgh and the Lothians, Stirlingshire, Falkirk and Tayside, Aberdeen and Aberdeenshire; about 22 of 46 are in Scotland by the homepage counts). Several brands sit under it (Corner House, Holyrood, Happitots, The Mere). The crawl spent its budget on English nursery pages (Cheshire, Salford), so the **nursery-page findings are from Thrive Cotton Lane, Chester** and are an Ofsted and EYFS example. The Scottish rules (1,140 hours, Care Inspectorate) appear on the fees page and in homepage text. I did not crawl a Scottish Thrive nursery page.

## At a glance
- **Who:** large private group, "46 day nurseries and preschools", "birth to 11 years" (holiday clubs for 5 to 11). Some sites date back to 1989.
- **Price position:** mid to upper-mid, with an all-inclusive pitch (nappies, formula, wipes, meals, settling-in sessions, some weekly swimming and Spanish are included in some nurseries' fees according to parents). No fee figures are published: "Nursery fees are nursery specific and will be made available to you on request."
- **URL:** https://www.thrivechildcare.com/ (confirmed). Built and designed by an agency (credit in footer); Cookiebot consent; ParentZone and Tapestry apps.

## How it sells
- **Promise:** "Children first, every time." A professional and warm tone: "We know it's a big step", "Sneaking off without saying goodbye can leave some children anxious." The settling-in page is the most reassuring page in my whole sample.
- **Proof:** badges (daynurseries.co.uk "Recommended", "Top 20 Nursery Group", Investors in People Gold, Eco-Schools Green Flag); per-nursery review count and star rating on each nursery page; five-star reviews pulled onto each nursery page with the reviewer's relationship ("Mother of Child"); an Ofsted line written in prose ("rated Good in all areas"); Food Hygiene rating 5. Reviews on the homepage are first-name-and-initial, and several mention other brands (Happitots), so they feel borrowed from other nurseries.
- **Differentiators:** an all-in fee list with nappies, wipes, formula, meals, sun cream; free settling-in sessions (at least three); "physically active a minimum of 3 hours per day"; Forest School; mini farms at some sites; ParentZone app with photos and notes from the key person; extras like swimming, Spanish, ballet; graduation with "caps, gowns, the works"; delivery acceptance (parcels sent to the nursery); free downloadable play activities for home.
- **Urgency and scarcity:** moderate. "Places fill quickly, so we recommend visiting early." "You're welcome to visit before your baby is born." Open days four times a year with a date banner. No countdowns.

## Conversion mechanics
- **CTAs and where:** "Book a tour" is in the header on every page, as a floating bottom-right pill on desktop, in every nursery row, at the top of each nursery page, at the end of the settling-in page, on open-days and in the footer areas. "Find a nursery" is the other header button. The freephone number is in the header.
- **Tour flow:** (1) find a nursery (postcode search or region accordion), (2) open the nursery page or the main Book a Nursery Tour page, (3) one form: first name, last name, email, child's first name, child's last name, child's date of birth, preferred start date, area, nursery, message, GDPR consent tick-box, submit. It is a single step, no calendar. (4) "A member of our team will be in touch to arrange a convenient time." The tour page spells out what a tour includes (welcome, rooms, outdoor area, meet the team, daily routine, policies, your questions) and answers "Is there any obligation? None at all."
- **Enquiry form on nursery pages:** a second form asking first name, last name, email, phone and "How did you hear about us?", plus a "request a call back".
- **Open days:** register interest (area, nursery, how did you hear about us).
- **Fees transparency:** low. Fee amounts are on request; the page lists what the fee includes and how to pay (Tax-Free Childcare, vouchers, the ParentZone app, direct debit; no cash or cheque).
- **Funding:** for Scotland the page says 3 and 4 year olds may be eligible for up to 1,140 hours, and the group "stretches" funded hours over the whole year to "up to 22 hours a week", and mentions caveats on cross-boundary funding. For England it explains 15 and 30 hour entitlements and eligibility from 9 months. The homepage headline "We accept Government funding for 9 months, 2yrs and 3yrs" is an England claim shown to Scottish visitors too; confusing.
- **Waiting list:** none mentioned beyond "subject to availability".
- **Pop-ups:** Cookiebot consent (all toggles pre-ticked on screen) and a floating "Book a tour" button.

## Visual design
- **Type:** Omnes Pro (Adobe Fonts), a rounded humanist sans, headings at weight 800 and body at 600. Not self-hosted (Typekit), which our GDPR rule forbids.
- **Palette (ink colour from computed styles, the tints estimated by eye from screenshots, so approximate):** near-black navy ink (#111921), sage green about #96AF9F and mint (#B8DDD0, #E5EFE6) for buttons and bands, salmon or blush (#F4DAD3, #F8E6E3) for the second CTA and bands, aqua (#B0DCD8), sand (#E0D9D0 or similar). It is a soft pastel set with one dark text colour, no primary colours.
- **Shapes:** pill buttons, big circle photo masks with a smaller overlapping circle in a tint, rounded cards with soft shadow, speech-bubble tails on review cards, line-drawn leaves and seedlings as decoration. Section bands alternate (white, blush, mint).
- **Photography:** professional-looking lifestyle photography of toddlers and staff; many children are identifiable and close-up (one huge smiling portrait is repeated). Warm, natural light. Cheerful but "stocky".
- **Motion:** review slider and a fixed floating CTA.
- **Density:** generous space on the homepage, but nursery pages are very long (3,000 to 4,000 words) with 15-question FAQs.
- **Fun:** circle photo masks with an overlapping bubble; coloured stage cards; leaf line art.
- **Premium:** the muted pastel palette with one dark ink colour; calm type.
- **Generic:** the nursery-page template is identical across 46 sites; a lot of repeated text; empty overlapping circles ("bubble") on the photos look like a missing image.

## Phone experience
- **Works:** big tap targets; the stage cards and accordion rows stack well; the "what's included" tick list is easy to scan; the phone number is in the header; opening hours are in a simple table; the tour form is a single column.
- **Doesn't:** the Cookiebot panel fills the screen at first load; in the desktop screenshot the open About Us and Your Child sub-menu panels sit on top of the hero text (a hover or focus state captured by the screenshot tool, so possibly an artefact); nursery pages are very long with no jump menu; the homepage shows England-only funding wording to Scottish parents; no fee numbers.

## Steal this
1. **The "What's included in the fee" tick list.** Nappies, wipes, meals, settling-in sessions and so on. Only list what the nursery confirms. Illustrate with simple drawn icons, not children.
2. **A "What happens on a tour" panel with "Is there any obligation? None at all."** This removes fear from the booking step. Use it above the form on our enquiry page.
3. **Stage cards in four pastel tints (Babies, Toddlers, Preschool).** Each card gets a colour, an age range and one sentence. On our builds use a photo of the room, a toy basket, or a children's drawing instead of a child's face.
4. **A settling-in page written to the parent's emotions.** "We know it's a big step... Letting your child see you smile during handover... Saying goodbye before you leave will build trust." Adapt with the nursery's own settling-in steps and a 4-step visual timeline.
5. **Circle photo masks with an overlapping tinted circle.** A friendly shape that frames a garden, a mud kitchen or a pair of wellies and avoids faces; make sure the second circle holds something (a leaf drawing or a number) rather than staying empty.
6. **A tour form that asks for child date of birth and preferred start date.** This lets the nursery answer "do we have a place" on the first call. Add "phone to check" copy where availability is unclear.
7. **Free downloadable "play at home" sheets** (tummy time, painting with water). A cheap, useful goodwill gesture; build from the nursery's own activities with hand-only photos.
8. **A floating "Book a tour" on desktop and a sticky bar on phone**, with the phone number beside it.

## Avoid this
- Hiding fees; "on request" fails the "What will it cost?" top task in our research.
- England-only funding claims shown to Scottish visitors.
- Reviews that name another brand or sit on a nursery that isn't the visitor's.
- Third-party fonts (Typekit) and Cookiebot with all categories pre-ticked; our rule is self-hosted fonts and no pop-ups.
- 4,000-word nursery pages with 15 FAQs and no jump menu.
- Decorative empty circles that look like missing images.
- Close-up portraits of identifiable children (we cannot reuse them).
