## At a glance
- **Who:** an outdoor nursery. Willow Den is a limited company and a wholly owned subsidiary of Inspiring Scotland (a charity); the Edinburgh setting is at Spartans' Ainslie Park football stadium, EH5, for ages 3 to primary school. Care Inspectorate registration CS2021000369. It also mentions a Glasgow City Council partnership and a "Thrive Outdoors" team.
- **Price position:** funded-first. "Funded Partner of City of Edinburgh Council": full-year (50 week) funded places; extra private hours at **£70.00 a day** (8am to 5pm), "from £292" a month per day of the week, fees from 1 August 2026. Includes a two-course hot meal, two snacks, waterproofs (for 2 or more sessions a week), a 10% sibling discount.
- **URL:** https://www.willowden.scot/ (confirmed). Website by Hillside Agency. The footer says 2021, though content is current (2026/27 fees).

## How it sells
- **Promise:** outdoor all day, in all weathers, as a right: "Where Children Thrive Outdoors", "so that all of Scotland's children can thrive outdoors", "Together, we are Willow Den".
- **Tone:** an earnest, mission-led voice that borrows the language of policy (UNCRC Article 31, National Outdoor Play Position Statement, Realising the Ambition) alongside poetic quotes (John Muir; David Sobel: "let us allow them to love the earth before we ask them to save it"). Warm for professionals and informed parents, a bit heavy for an anxious first-time parent. The most charming line is on the nursery page: "a hidden garden through our 'secret gate'".
- **Real phrases:** "Like tasting rain, building dens, creating artwork with natural resources"; "A pirate ship can become a dinosaur in the blink of an eye"; "Squiggles and mark making help to develop early literacy. Children learn how to tell stories... through lines, circles and wiggly things."; "Children can see some of Scotland's top female sporting athletes in training."
- **Proof:** Funded Partner status; the inspection report is a PDF on the page (the site does not state grades in text); a Care Inspectorate number in the footer; the Spartans partnership; named staff with qualifications and years of experience; policies published in full; the Spotty Otter waterproof clothing partner brand ("kitted out at low or no cost").
- **Differentiators:** fully outdoor; a stadium setting; Forest Fridays; a clothing kit included; the ability to split funded hours; nappies accepted ("My child is still wearing nappies, can they still attend?" is a FAQ); holiday clubs; the national outdoor-learning movement.
- **Urgency and scarcity:** "We are now taking registrations for 2026/27 – get in touch today", the green "Register today!" bar, registration eligibility by date of birth (born 1 March 2023 to 29 February 2024 means a funded place in 2026/27), "pending availability" on Forest Fridays.

## Conversion mechanics
- **CTAs:** "Register today!" (top bar on every page), "Enquire Now" (nursery page, top and bottom), "Pre-register now", "Book a visit", "MORE" buttons. Phone 0131 376 0080 is on the contact page and nursery footer; no click-to-call in the header.
- **Enquiry flow:** a pre-registration form and an enquiry form (both embedded and not captured), then a "Visit the Nursery" box inviting parents to a "cosy welcome cabin" to meet the manager (named), with a booking form with time slots ("If you cannot find a date a time slot to suit you in the form below, please contact...").
- **Fees transparency:** high. A table with day rate (£70.00), hours and "from £292" a month, with the date they apply from, a menu PDF, and the inclusions list. On a phone the table is narrow and squeezed.
- **Funding clarity:** high. Which birth dates get a funded place which year; 50-week funded places; split placements; Tax-Free Childcare and vouchers.
- **Waiting list:** pre-registration acts as one, the wording is not explicit.
- **Pop-ups:** a small cookie box (Accept or Cookie settings) bottom right; no chat.

## Visual design
- **Type:** Vollkorn (a warm book serif) at weight 700 for headings in forest green (about #3F6052); Source Sans Pro 18px for body. Terracotta (about #DCA674) italic serif for pull quotes and links.
- **Palette:** forest green #3F6052 text and bars, cream page ground #F4F3F0, terracotta/clay #DCA674, olive #98AC4B for icons and buttons, mustard yellow, teal and orange in illustration.
- **Illustration:** the signature. A flat vector autumn-leaf set with a stippled texture, in a consistent palette, framing the hero as a full ring, overlapping photos, and as blob icons. Yellow swallow-like birds. Foot-print marks as a decorative element (animal tracks) on the Why Outdoors page.
- **Shapes:** organic blob masks on photos (a leaf shape, a pill, a wavy panel edge), pill buttons, the leaf in the logo.
- **Photography:** professional film stills (credited), close and joyful, children's faces visible and in motion (laughing with mud, throwing paint). Also hands and leaves. Gorgeous but would need consent for every child.
- **Motion:** minimal; a leaf marker on the active menu item.
- **Density:** open, long single columns with big margins; the "Why outdoors" pages are text-heavy.
- **Fun:** the leaf frame, the blob photo masks, the secret gate idea, mud and paint.
- **Premium:** the serif and green on cream.
- **Twee or weak:** the border of leaves is a lot at 390px (it eats a third of the first screen); some pages are long essays on policy; the fee table breaks words on a phone ("Provisio / n", "Monthl / y").

## Phone experience
- **Works:** big type; the green announcement bar is a clear call to act; the cream and green palette is calm; sections stack cleanly.
- **Doesn't:** the first screen is mostly decoration (the leaf ring) with no phone number, hours or address; the fees table wraps mid-word; many downloads are PDFs named like files ("LL_SS26_Standard Menu Pack_Lunch_PLATINUM"); FAQ answers are hidden; the cookie box sits over the hero image.

## Steal this
1. **A hero framed by a repeating hand-drawn pattern.** Commission or have the children make a leaf-print, handprint or potato-print border and use it as a frame around the headline. No faces needed; the nursery's own children's prints do the job.
2. **"The secret gate" story.** A one-line magical entrance (a gate, a door, a path) with a photo of the gate or garden with no one in it. Ask each nursery for one thing that feels like a secret.
3. **Blob and pill photo masks.** Put rooms, kit and hands in organic shapes; it signals play without clip art and also crops out faces naturally.
4. **Funded-eligibility by date of birth.** "Born 1 March 2023 to 29 February 2024: funded place in 2026/27." Specific, useful, fact-checkable (always confirm with the council and date it).
5. **"Included" list that includes the kit.** Waterproofs, meals, snacks. Show the actual kit laid out on pegs and boots in a row, no children.
6. **Name the manager and give her a human line.** The bio includes a personal detail (cooking with her four children outdoors). Use a staff-consented portrait or a hand-drawn portrait with a short real bio.
7. **A short, visible "Register today!" bar tied to a real date window.** Only use it where the nursery has real dates; otherwise use "Ask about places".
8. **A visit as an invitation to a named, cosy place ("our welcome cabin") with the manager's name.** Reuse for any nursery: name the room where the tour starts.

## Avoid this
- Heavy decorative framing that pushes the key facts (phone, hours, funded status) off the first phone screen.
- A phone fee table that breaks words; use a stacked card list at 390px.
- Essay-length policy copy for first-time parents (UNCRC, UN goals).
- Hiding FAQ answers behind unscannable accordions with no summary.
- Face-forward action photos as the core visual; we can't reuse them.
- PDF file names and a 2021 copyright left showing.
