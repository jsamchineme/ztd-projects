## At a glance
- **Who:** a large UK group founded in 2008. The About page says 18+ years, 9,000+ team members, 40,000+ children and "350+ nurseries internationally". Nurseries are in England (the featured ones are Bolton, Oxford, Birmingham, Northwich). Scotland appears only in the funding text. No Scottish nursery appeared in the featured list.
- **Position:** mid to premium private chain ("Exceptional early years education"). It publishes an "Essentials & Extras" fee table (see below) but not nursery-by-nursery fees.
- **Recognition:** Best Workplaces badges and a daynurseries.co.uk "Top 20 Nursery Group 2026" award in the footer.
- **Built by:** Studio Graft (credit in the footer). This is a 2026-era design and the most modern of our five.

## How it sells
- **Promise:** "Where curiosity takes root" (hero), "Exploring the world, one step at a time", "The people behind every great start". Quiet and outdoorsy, with a natural-world metaphor (roots, planet, world).
- **Tone:** warm and confident, not baby-talk. Mid-length sentences, plain English. "Every story shared, friendship formed, muddy puddle jumped in, question asked and challenge overcome helps children grow in confidence and curiosity." "It's not just what children learn. It's how they learn."
- **Proof:**
  - A **9.5 / 13,254 review** score from daynurseries.co.uk, shown in the hero, with live-dated parent reviews (every review carries a date, a nursery and "Mother/Father of Child"). Only 4- and 5-star reviews are displayed ("Displaying our 4 & 5 star reviews"), and the page says so.
  - Awards (Best Workplaces, a daynurseries.co.uk Top 20 group).
  - Each nursery page links to the latest Ofsted report (a dark navy card: "Latest Ofsted Report - view our most recent inspection report on the Ofsted website") rather than quoting the grade.
  - Safety facts on nursery pages: secure entry system (keypad and intercom), paediatric first aid on every shift, key person system.
- **Differentiators:** Udeskole outdoor learning ("our own award-winning" approach), the Famly parent app (photos, observations and booking extra sessions), freshly prepared meals, a "Kids Planet Pedagogy", all-inclusive fees (a feature chip on nursery pages), flexible sessions. "1,000 Little Adventures Challenge" appears in the news.
- **Urgency or scarcity:** only the "Register for open day today" strip. No "places left" counter.

## Conversion mechanics
- **CTAs:** the orange strip (open day), "Book a Tour" in the sticky header, "Find Nurseries" in the hero, "Book a Tour" on every nursery card, a final yellow "Find a Nursery". On a nursery page a dark panel "Enquire about a place" holds three actions: "Book a tour", "Register your child", "Email the nursery", and the note "Your details are secure. We never share your info."
- **Tour flow (global form at `/book-a-tour/`):** a short intro ("Tell us which nursery you'd like to visit...") and a three-step "How it works" panel: 01 Request received ("Your tour isn't booked just yet"), 02 We'll be in touch (the Nursery Manager will contact you), 03 Come and explore. Fields: nursery, first name, last name, email, mobile (+44 with a flag), child first name and surname, child date of birth ("please estimate if your little one hasn't arrived yet"), child gender, "When are you looking to start?" ("if you are unsure, an estimate is fine"), a free-text box about sessions, funding, allergies and needs, reCAPTCHA, "Submit". It is a single page, 10 or so fields, no calendar and no phone number on the form. Submissions go to HubSpot. The "your tour isn't booked just yet" line sets honest expectations.
- **Fees:** the best fee transparency of the five. It never shows nursery fees, but it shows a clear **Essentials & Extras table** (full day £10.00 = meals £7, consumables £2, activities £1; half day £5.00; short day £8.00), with a worked example and an "opt out" explanation. Nursery pages carry an "All-Inclusive Fees" chip and a Fees & Funding Guide per nursery.
- **Funding:** separate England, Scotland and Wales explanations. For Scotland: "All three and four-year-olds receive 1,140 hours of funded Early Learning and Childcare, regardless of whether their parents are working."
- **Opening hours:** shown as a card on each nursery page with a green "Open" chip, Monday to Friday 8:00am to 6:00pm, closed at the weekend.
- **Chat or pop-ups:** a Cookiebot banner that covers most of the first phone screen. No chat widget seen.
- **Waiting list:** "Register your child" on each nursery page. Not explained further in our crawl.

## Visual design
- **Typefaces:** "Urbane" (a friendly geometric sans with slightly quirky letters, from Adobe Fonts) for everything: headings at 700, body at 500, headline sizes around 32 to 48px. The H1s are cream (`#FFF5EB`) on photos. One family used well, with no serif.
- **Palette:** deep indigo navy `#282145` (text and headings), a warm cream `#FFF5EB` ground, a dusty periwinkle blue `#4873B8` for buttons and the footer, a burnt orange-red strip (about `#C8451E`), mustard yellow band, sage/olive green panels, a blush pink panel. Together these feel like a warm gouache palette and not primary colours.
- **Illustration:** cut-paper shapes with rough torn edges: trophy, bulb, shield, magnifier, hand, question mark, map pin. The same torn edge is used as a divider between sections and on the bottom of photos. This is the most distinctive thing on the site. It looks handmade and playful but is applied with restraint.
- **Photography:** real lifestyle shoots, children's faces shown. A mix: an out-of-focus close-up portrait in the hero, children in hi-vis vests outdoors, a baby asleep from above, a table of children eating with an adult, a staff member with children. Nursery cards use plain exterior photos of the actual buildings (brick houses, car park), which look honest and unglamorous.
- **Icons:** small outline icons (eye, calendar, map pin, clock) on buttons, plus the larger paper-cut set. Many pill buttons with an icon on the right.
- **Shapes and textures:** pill buttons, 12px rounded cards with soft shadow, torn-paper dividers, a frosted-glass search panel.
- **Motion:** card photo carousels and a review swipe row. No other motion noted.
- **Density:** airy and well-ordered. Large type, generous space.
- **Fun and engaging:** the paper-cut icons, torn edges, the colour blocks (yellow, green, pink panels on nursery pages).
- **Premium:** the single typeface, the cream and navy pairing, the restrained photography.
- **Twee or generic:** the repeated "eye" icon on every "View" button, a review box with only "..." in it, placeholder house icon on one card.

## Phone experience
- **Works:** one column, big tap targets, the full-width blue pill buttons, a search box in the thumb zone, accordion footer, torn dividers that make a long page feel like chapters. Tour form fields are large with helper text under the awkward ones (phone number, date of birth, start date).
- **Doesn't:** the cookie dialog covers the whole first view on every page, so the hero is hidden. The review carousel clips cards at the right edge. The nursery page is very long (6 or more screens), and the team-photo block was empty in our capture. The orange strip plus a header use up 100px of the first screen.

## Steal this
1. **Torn-paper section edges.** A wobbly cut line between a cream and a coloured section is easy in CSS (clip-path or an SVG mask) and gives a handmade feel with no photos. We can draw our own, or take the edge from the nursery's own painted paper.
2. **Cut-paper icons in place of stock illustration.** Use three or four simple shapes (sun, boot, apple, key) in two or three flat colours with rough edges. They can stand in for children in "How it works" and "Why us" rows.
3. **A "how it works" strip on the tour form that is honest:** "Request received. Your tour isn't booked just yet. The manager will contact you. Come and explore." Copy the honesty. Never promise a time slot we can't guarantee.
4. **A full fee table with a worked example.** "Full day £10, half day £5" with an opt-out line. For a small nursery, publish the real fee sheet with a date, and where unsure write "phone to check". Never invent prices.
5. **Plain Scottish funding line.** Kids Planet's "All three and four-year-olds receive 1,140 hours ... regardless of whether their parents are working" is a good model. We should write it, then add our own funded-places and 2-year-old detail after checking with the nursery and the local council.
6. **Facts as tags on a card:** "Early start", "Late finish", "On-site parking", "Freshly prepared meals". For a one-nursery site, a "Good to know" chip row under the hero, each chip backed by a real fact from the nursery.
7. **Opening hours card with an "Open" chip.** Our Front Desk families already do this live. Reuse it for nurseries.
8. **Audience toggle in the top bar ("I'm a parent / I want a career").** A small tab pair that keeps recruitment out of the parents' way.
9. **Torn-edge photo bottoms and a frosted-glass search or enquiry panel.** With no children's faces: use the garden, the cloakroom, painted pegs, a table laid for lunch, small hands on clay.
10. **Parents' reviews with the source, date and role** ("Mother of child, 13 Oct 2026"). Only use if the nursery gives permission and the source is linkable. Our version should say where each one comes from and not filter silently ("Displaying our 4 & 5 star reviews" is at least disclosed).

## Avoid this
- A cookie wall over the hero. We use no trackers, so no banner.
- A scrolling review box with "..." or clipped cards. Our rules ban carousels anyway.
- Sending every enquiry to one global form that asks for the child's gender and surname before the parent has even seen a room. Ask the minimum (name, phone or email, child's age).
- Displaying only 4 and 5 star reviews as "What parents say" with a headline score from 13,000 reviews across many nurseries. Parents can't tell which nursery the score belongs to. A small nursery should show its own count and source.
- A "9.5 out of 10" disc that borrows a third-party scale. Say "9.5 from daynurseries.co.uk" every time.
- Placeholder icons where a photo should be.
- A hero that is a close-up of one identifiable child. We can't reuse it.
