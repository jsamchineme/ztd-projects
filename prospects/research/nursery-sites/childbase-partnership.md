## How it sells
- **Promise:** "Delivering childcare excellence since 1989", "Fun is an essential ingredient in every Childbase Partnership nursery". "Places available. Contact us today."
- **Tone:** formal and corporate on the homepage ("redefine excellence in every area of operation"), friendlier on nursery pages ("There is never a dull day at Woodlands Day Nursery").
- **Proof:**
  - **League table:** "No.1 in the Nursery World quality league table, three years running", based on Ofsted results, with an honest caveat about Ofsted's change from single-word judgements.
  - A **Ofsted report** button on every nursery page.
  - **9.9 from 6,295 daynurseries reviews** (group), and each nursery page shows its own (for example Woodlands: 9.4 from 102 reviews).
  - Employee ownership and "Best Companies" workplace awards, Green Flag with distinction for all nurseries three years running, Investors in Diversity, Princess Royal Training Award.
- **Differentiators:**
  - **Fully inclusive fees** (formula milk, nappies, cream, wipes, sun cream, meals, snacks and all activities). Parents can opt in, opt out or mix ("consumables are voluntary").
  - Eco-Schools Green Flag in every nursery, Eco Committees led by pre-schoolers, forest schools, a net-zero target by 2045.
  - **Childbase@Home:** a members-only family website and app (activity videos, workshops, parent training: first aid, school readiness, speech and language).
  - Ready, Steady, Grow curriculum, with a parent guide for the final year before school.
  - Room names: Caterpillars, Ladybirds, Bumblebees, Preschool. Sing and Sign, Boogie Mites music sessions.
  - Named nursery manager with qualifications and years of service ("Manager at Woodlands for 10 years... BA (Hons) and EYPS").
  - A what3words address on each nursery page (for example Woodlands: plenty.rebounder.piston) and parking details.
- **Urgency or scarcity:** "Places available. Contact us today." as a hero slide, and "Opening Jan 2027" tags for new nurseries.

## Phone experience
- **Works:** short, one-column; big tap targets for the nursery action stack ("Fees and Funding", "Our Menu", "Our Ofsted Report", "Enquiry / Book a Tour") (the four most useful actions, one per button); the postcode finder is big and simple; the "No.1" line is visible within the first screen.
- **Doesn't:** the burger is the only navigation, so there is no visible "Book a tour" button in the header; the curriculum page is a tiny embedded flip-book ("Loading PDF 18%"), which is unreadable on a phone; centred light body text; news cards with blank image gaps; long text blocks on the nursery page (2,277 words) with few breaks; homepage "Words: 0" means there's no real text for a search engine or a screen reader on the hero slider.

## Steal this
1. **A hand-drawn flourish around the one number that matters.** The yellow marker ring round "No.1" is cheap, human and memorable. For us: ring the real date of the last Care Inspectorate report, "Open since 1998" or "7.30am to 6pm", drawn as a thin, imperfect SVG line that also works for an anxious parent's eye.
2. **A four-button action stack on the nursery page:** Fees and funding · Menu · Inspection report · Enquire or book a tour. It answers the four questions every parent has. Copy the stack, in graduated colours from our palette, as full-width buttons on a phone.
3. **Fee factsheet per nursery, downloadable, dated.** Pair it with a short on-page table. If the nursery won't publish fees, say "phone to check" and give the number.
4. **Honest wording about inspections.** Childbase notes the change in Ofsted's framework. We should do the same for Scotland: show the Care Inspectorate grades by theme, with the date, and a link to the report. Never invent a grade.
5. **Room names that are creatures.** Caterpillars, Ladybirds, Bumblebees, Preschool. Use the nursery's real room names as the "Rooms by age" cards, each with a small illustration, the ages and the staff team (with consent).
6. **A manager's welcome with real credentials and years of service** ("Manager for 10 years, in the nursery for 14"). It is the easiest personal touch for a small nursery, and parents trust it. Use a name and a handwritten sign-off only with consent, and no photo if we can't have one.
7. **Opt-in, opt-out and mix-and-match for consumables.** The three-option layout is a clear way to explain what's included and what's optional. Use only if the nursery's actual policy is like this.
8. **A what3words address and parking line** on the contact block. For rural Scottish nurseries, a what3words code, a "turn at the red gate" note and a parking line help a first-time visitor.
9. **A hero image that shows an activity with no faces:** a hand drawing in wet sand next to yellow cups and a toy octopus. It is warm, curious and anonymous. That is exactly what we can build from: hands, tools, textures and mess.
10. **A members-only family hub** ("Childbase@Home": videos, worksheets, workshops) can be reduced for a small nursery to "Try this at home" pages (a simple printable per month), no log-in.

## Avoid this
- A burger-only menu on desktop, and no visible "Book a tour" in the header.
- A flip-book PDF for the curriculum. Use plain headings and short text instead.
- Stock images (a piggy bank on the fees page).
- Light-weight, centred body text on cream.
- Corporate phrases ("redefine excellence in every area of operation").
- Blank image slots in the news list, and a slider as the first thing a parent sees.
- Photos of identifiable staff and children: we can't reuse these.
- Treating the Ofsted wording as if it applies in Scotland. We use the Care Inspectorate's quality themes, which are graded differently.
