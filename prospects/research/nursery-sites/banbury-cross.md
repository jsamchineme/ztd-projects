## How it sells
- **Promise:** home from home. "Happy and loved", "the care, attention, and love that they would receive at home". Cuddles, cosy corners, routines from home are followed.
- **Tone:** quiet, confident, affectionate, short. 684 words for the whole site is about a third of a Thrive nursery page. It trusts the awards, the photos and the reviews to do the work.
- **Real phrases:** "treasure baskets", "decked area in the garden", "walks in the buggies", "a sleep room which provides the babies with a relaxing environment", "we also take advantage of the local park, beach and woodland area".
- **Proof:** the NDNA Scotland 2026 award, called out twice; three parent testimonials, one including a sibling-move story ("we moved our eldest child from another local nursery... the best decision we made"); the "longstanding staff team"; a Care Inspectorate link. **Grades are not shown on the page** even though the link exists; a clear gap given how a parent is told to judge quality.
- **Differentiators:** babies from 3 months; beach and woodland outings; sleep room; small and family-run; award.
- **Urgency and scarcity:** none. "We would love for you to come along and visit us."

## Visual design
- **Type:** Mrs Eaves XL Serif (Adobe Typekit) in light sage for headings, with its narrow cut for small caps labels; Funnel Sans (Google Fonts) for body at 18px. Light, bookish and elegant. Headings are not bold, which is part of the premium feel.
- **Palette:** sage green about #597D71 (computed, the heading colour), warm cream page ground (about #F4F0E4, by eye), a teal-blue panel (about #437E94, by eye), a muted lilac (about #8A7CA8, by eye), a tan-brown tree in the logo (about #B08558), white cards. Soft, natural, no primary colours.
- **Illustration:** the logo only. The tree and a reading child is a lovely, ownable mark; no clip art anywhere else.
- **Shapes:** a **wavy edge** where the hero meets the page, 24px rounded cards with a gentle shadow, photos with an **offset coloured "shadow tile"** behind them, rounded pill buttons in sage.
- **Photography:** real photos, mostly phone-quality, warm and personal: a baby in a wicker basket, a child on grass in a unicorn coat laughing, a sleeping toddler on a carer's shoulder, children walking away in hi-vis on a beach, the award night. Faces are visible in many.
- **Motion:** Slick carousel (cdnjs) for reviews or gallery; nothing else seen.
- **Density:** airy, large type, tall sections; the whole page is about eight screens on a phone.
- **Fun:** the offset tile frames, the wavy hero edge, the colour-coded room panels.
- **Premium:** the type pairing, restraint, cream and sage, light-weight serif, tracked small caps.
- **Weak:** tall photos that crop faces awkwardly on a phone; the full-colour award badge sits on a busy photo; hero image has nothing about location or practical facts.

## Phone experience
- **Works:** readable at 18px, calm single column, solid colour panels make each age group a clear "chapter", photos are large and fast to understand, nothing overlaps.
- **Doesn't:** no visible menu or phone button after the first screen; no sticky call-to-action; practical answers (fees, funding, ratios, availability, grades) are missing; the Famly form takes the parent off-site.

## Steal this
1. **One-page "chapters" by age group, each in its own solid colour panel.** Colour-code Babies, Toddlers and Pre-school and repeat the colour in the tab bar. Photograph the room, a toy basket, a coat-peg row (no names), a hand on a treasure basket.
2. **A hand-drawn tree-and-child style logo as the single illustration.** For a small Scottish nursery, commission one simple ownable mark (their street tree, a local landmark) instead of clip art. Reuse it as a footer and favicon.
3. **Wavy hero edge and offset colour tile frames.** Pure CSS shapes that feel handmade and need no stock art.
4. **An "Award News" card** that credits the staff and families, not the building. Use it for any real award (and only real ones); for a nursery with none, use "Latest inspection: [grades, date, link]" in the same card style.
5. **Light serif plus a clean sans, headings not bold.** A premium voice that parents read as calm. Self-host both fonts.
6. **A very short page.** A 684-word site is easy to read for anxious parents. Pair it with the facts it omits: fees from [date], funded status, hours, grades.
7. **Location as a selling point, told with outings** ("park, beach and woodland close by"). Show it as a hand-drawn local map or a row of weather icons with the real places listed.
8. **Name the team's checks (PVG, SSSC)** in plain English, a trust cue that costs nothing. Only say it if the nursery confirms.

## Avoid this
- No fees, no funded-hours statement and no grades on the page. They are the first things a parent asks for.
- No header CTA, no menu on the phone and no sticky phone button.
- Sending the parent to an external app form without saying what it asks.
- Putting a full-colour award badge over a busy hero photo; use a clean panel.
- Faces cropped on tall mobile photos. Our builds will use no identifiable children, so frame objects, hands and rooms on purpose.
- Reliance on third-party fonts (Typekit, Google Fonts) and a CDN carousel; self-host and skip carousels.
