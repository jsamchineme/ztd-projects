# Little Forest Folk

## At a glance
- **Who:** a small London group of fully outdoor "forest nurseries" for children aged 2 to 5: Chiswick, Fulham, Morden, Twickenham, Wimbledon Village, Wimbledon and Winkworth (Surrey, opened 2025). Older testimonials mention Putney Heath, Barnes and Wandsworth. Open 51 weeks (Chiswick page).
- **Price position:** premium. Parents carry a waiting-list sign-up fee of 25 pounds (non-refundable) plus enrolment fee and deposit when a place is offered. Per-nursery fee pages and PDFs exist.
- **URL:** https://www.littleforestfolk.com/ (built on Squarespace). **Studied:** 13 Oct 2026. Crawl and screenshots in `study-1/littleforestfolk/`.
- **Proof headline:** Ofsted Outstanding (badge), Tatler's best nurseries, Queen's Award for Enterprise 2018, TES Early Years Setting of the Year 2019 (Wimbledon), Eco-Schools Green Flag (Chiswick, Putney Heath, Twickenham, Wimbledon), Bright Stars 2024, Merton Business Awards 2015, and a pull-quote from Sir David Attenborough.

## How it sells
- **Promise:** "Great childcare in the great outdoors". The nursery is the forest itself. The emotional hook is freedom and childhood the way adults remember it: "the wonderful outdoors childhood that many of us were fortunate enough to have experienced but that children in London are sadly now missing out on".
- **Tone:** lyrical but specific, with parent-to-parent warmth ("As parents ourselves"), occasional humour, a few clever phrases: "forest readiness", "Wild Cooking Fridays", "our Forest Folk-ers", "mud from their nose to their finger tips", "Wisdom begins in wonder".
- **Proof:** the awards row; an Attenborough quote (his words about nature, placed as endorsement; I could not tell whether he endorsed the nursery directly, so do not copy this pattern); long dated parent testimonials (2019 to 2022 visible) signed by nursery; the Evidence page of research references.
- **Differentiators:** fully outdoor, in real parks and estates (Chiswick House and Gardens, Fulham Palace, Wimbledon Common, a National Trust arboretum, a 3.5-hectare reserve); pop-up sleeping, changing and toilet tents; colour-coded waterproofs by age group (high-visibility); safety (staff walkie-talkies, a stated ratio, headcounts); chef-cooked food by an outside caterer (Two Teaspoons); Famly app with a daily unposed photo; holiday camps.
- **Urgency and scarcity:** strong. "Limited availability" banner; a waiting list with a 12 to 24 month aim; "we can never make a guarantee"; main intakes January, April, September; tour tickets released to those on the list; "please wait until your new arrival has arrived safely" before signing up (pregnant parents). The nursery says no to people, which makes it feel desirable.

## Conversion mechanics
- **CTAs:** a coral "Learn More" in the hero (goes to the story). The real CTA is the stack of seven dark teal "Enquire about joining [nursery]" buttons, repeated on home, enquiries and each nursery page. Tour booking is through free Eventbrite tickets released per nursery. A mailing-list sign-up (email only) on most pages.
- **Enquiry flow:** the button opens an external form for the chosen nursery (not completed). Chosen nursery first, then details, then pay the 25 pound waiting-list fee (from the FAQ). You can join up to 2 nursery lists for one fee. Siblings of enrolled children get priority by email.
- **Fees:** each nursery page links to its own fees page or PDF; no figures in the page text I crawled, apart from the waiting-list fee.
- **Pop-ups:** small cookie bar; announcement bar.

## Visual design
- **Type:** Proxima Nova (Adobe Fonts; weights 300 to 700) for everything; headings uppercase with 2px letter-spacing, about 22px; Adobe Garamond Pro is loaded but barely used. The hero headline is heavy white caps.
- **Palette:** coral `#E3564C` (header, headings, links), deep forest teal `#093C3E` (buttons, banners, footer), white, with grey-green `#A0B0B2`. A restricted, confident two-colour identity that is neither primary-colour nor pastel.
- **Illustration and identity:** a bold pine-tree emblem, a small owl emblem on videos, and a pawprint on a pale-gold tile. Simple flat icons. No clip art.
- **Photography:** full-bleed outdoor photos, many with identifiable children in high-visibility waterproofs, plus children from behind in long grass (the Learning page hero is a toddler from behind in a meadow, the best photo on the site). Also children in autumn leaves from a distance.
- **Motion:** minimal (slider arrows). A Squarespace site.
- **Density:** open, text-led, with long pages of copy.
- **Fun:** the colour of the waterproofs against green; the playful names; the nature photography. **Premium:** the restrained two-colour palette, the confident uppercase type. **Generic:** the Squarespace footer, the grey-green social footer, the very long testimonial and staff pages, a cookie bar in plain black.

## Phone experience
- **Works:** one column, big photos, the card-per-nursery stack, big dark enquiry buttons (full width, well above 44px), readable 18px copy.
- **Doesn't:** the black cookie bar sits over the middle of the first scroll and takes the headline; a hero line in thin white type over a busy meadow is hard to read on a phone; homepage is long (seven location cards, then seven enquiry buttons); the nursery pages are text walls; I saw no phone number in the page header.

## Steal this
1. **A two-colour identity with a bold emblem** (one warm colour, one deep neutral). For a Scottish nursery: choose a colour from their sign or garden, plus a deep neutral, and make a simple emblem from a real feature (a tree, a gate).
2. **"What a day looks like" told as a story** with named places and times (arrival, snack, outdoors, lunch, rest, home). Only as the nursery tells us. Show it on a vertical timeline and illustrate with the garden, kit and food, not faces.
3. **Colour-coded outdoor gear as a visual system.** For a Scottish nursery, show wellies and waterproofs lined up on pegs, no children, with a short "what to bring" list. Great for the Scottish weather.
4. **A real "limited availability" banner** that the nursery updates. Only if true, with the term named ("Autumn 2026").
5. **Waiting list explained in a plain FAQ**: how it works, how long, who has priority, what the fee covers, and "please wait until baby has arrived". The honesty builds trust. Only state what the nursery tells us.
6. **A voice with named playful terms** that the nursery chooses ("forest readiness", "Wild Cooking Fridays" are theirs, not ours). Ask the nursery for two or three of their own phrases.
7. **A "what to bring" kit list page** with a photo of the items laid out (no children).
8. **Free tour tickets**, one hour, one parent per child, released to the waiting list. Works as "Open morning" slots for a small nursery.
9. **The "meet the team" page with long personal bios.** With staff consent only; keep each to two sentences.

## Avoid this
- Eventbrite or other third-party ticketing (external cookies); use a form that sends an email or a phone number.
- Thin white text over a busy photo.
- 5,000-word testimonial pages; pick three short quotes with permission, or use none.
- Attenborough-style endorsements that we cannot verify.
- Photos of identifiable children in high-visibility vests (we cannot reuse the nursery's consent).
- Fees hidden behind PDFs.
