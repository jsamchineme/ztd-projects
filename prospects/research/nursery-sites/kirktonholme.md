# Kirktonholme Childcare (Kirktonholme Nurseries)

Studied 13 October 2026 by crawl (25 pages) and screenshots at 390px and 1280px. Screenshots and crawl output are in `study-3/kirktonholme/` in the session scratchpad (not in the repo).

## At a glance
- **Who:** family-owned group, first nursery opened May 1994; 11 nurseries across Glasgow, Lanarkshire, East Renfrewshire, East Dunbartonshire and Ayrshire (Airdrie, Bearsden, Broomhill, Coatbridge, East Kilbride, Govan, Mearnswood, Shettleston, Thornliebank, Tollcross, Wishaw). Head office East Kilbride.
- **Price position:** mid-market private. Published day rates are about £76 to £79 for a full day (0 to 3) in the nurseries that list fees, with morning and afternoon sessions about £42 to £48.
- **URL:** https://www.kirktonholmenursery.com/ (confirmed, real group; the footer still says 2021).
- **Site builder:** FullStop (credit in footer). CookieYes consent banner. Famly (parent app) listed among partner logos.

## How it sells
- **Promise:** a family, not a chain. "Welcome to The Kirktonholme Family". Attachment-led practice. "Plenty of cuddles throughout the day." "We ensure that children don't feel they have been dropped off with strangers."
- **Tone:** warm, a little formal, long sentences, some jargon (attachment led practice, looking inwards, outwards and forwards, Pre-Birth to Three, Realising the Ambition). Parent Club's research favours plainer wording than this.
- **Real phrases worth noting:**
  - "stay and play sessions with parents present... could take days, weeks or even months."
  - "There is no such thing as bad weather, only bad clothing" (framed as a moto, with a prompt to bring waterproofs).
  - "Our fantastic cook Agata freshly prepares a nutritious meal each day... Thai green curry, fish pie and chicken fajitas." A named cook and real dishes is a very good trust detail.
  - "Baby cottage, the Fern room": rooms have names and personalities (Fern, Thistle, Heather).
- **Proof:** over 25 years; Nursery Management Today awards (Group of the Year 2015, Best Outdoor Environments); a GIA design award nomination for the purpose-built Govan nursery (2018); ran the creche of the Scottish Parliament for 10 years; staff who have stayed 26 years; the group says it was first in Scotland to register an outdoor learning environment (their claim, not checked). Grades are claimed in words ("mostly 5's and 6's") with no per-nursery grades or dates on the page. Only Coatbridge links to its Care Inspectorate record.
- **Reviews:** two per nursery page, anonymous first name and initial; one on the homepage. Both Airdrie quotes are about settling in at the child's pace. This is the best content on the site.
- **Differentiators:** woodlands to seaside outdoor spaces, home-cooked meals, a baby cottage with its own garden and lie-flat prams for outdoor sleeping, trainee-to-manager career path, Famly parent app.
- **Urgency and scarcity:** none. No waiting list wording and no limited-places language.

## Conversion mechanics
- **CTAs:** "Enquire Now" (hero, goes to the contact form) and "Download Our Brochure" (hero). Each nursery page has a "Contact Us" button near the top and a Contact Us section at the bottom with phone and email.
- **Enquiry flow:** one step. Form fields: full name, email, nursery (dropdown), message, reCAPTCHA. No phone number or child age is asked, no calendar, no tour booking. Phone numbers are per nursery on the nursery page.
- **Fees:** published per nursery as a small table (Daily, Morning, Afternoon), 0 to 3 only; funded hours stated as one line ("up to 1140 hours"). This is more transparent than most groups. There is no mention of Tax-Free Childcare, what's included, or sibling discounts.
- **Funding page:** names six councils and links to them. It doesn't say which of those councils each nursery is a funded provider for.
- **Pop-ups:** a full-width consent banner (CookieYes) that sits over the hero on every page load. No chat widget.

## Visual design
- **Type:** Nunito at weight 900 for headings (rounded, friendly), Poppins for body (14px at the body, 18px for some paragraphs), and a Playfair-style high-contrast serif only in the logo wordmark.
- **Palette:** navy about #1D2C68 for headings and footer, orange about #F05A21 for buttons and the hamburger, with yellow, pink, teal and sky blue in the brush strokes and top stripe. Pale blue hero ground.
- **Illustration:** the strongest asset is the hand-painted watercolour rainbow strokes used as page dividers and as a faint decoration that runs behind text. Faint line-drawn doodles (stars, houses, children) sit behind the reviews section.
- **Shapes:** curved wave section edges, organic curved image masks (the review photo has a curved edge), pill buttons with a stitched dashed outline, cards with soft shadow, a coloured underline highlight under H2s (pink, green).
- **Photography:** a mix of stock-looking images on the homepage (the hero child at play-dough, two girls at a puzzle) and lots of real, small, phone-camera photos on the nursery pages: rooms, gardens and outdoor kit, but also many identifiable babies and toddlers' faces. Photo quality varies a lot. Alt text on the nursery index is good and descriptive (an unusual plus for accessibility).
- **Motion:** logo and review sliders, a marquee-style moving review text. Nothing else.
- **Density:** open and airy at the top, very long text walls on the nursery pages.
- **What feels fun:** the rainbow brush strokes and the rounded headings. They give warmth without clip-art.
- **What feels premium:** the logo and the navy and orange pairing.
- **What feels cheap or generic:** stock photos in the hero, the dashed stitched buttons, a broken video box, the 2021 copyright, long gallery grids of small phone photos.

## Phone experience
- **Works:** the hero heading is large and readable; the navy and orange has strong contrast; the footer stacks cleanly; the hours are in the footer.
- **Doesn't:** the cookie banner takes the first screen; the hero photo is lost behind a pale blue wash; a header with an address wraps to two lines; the review slider cuts text off on both sides ("I would highly recommend Kirkto...") so it is unreadable; the awards slider shows partial logos; the phone number isn't a sticky or top item; no click-to-call on the homepage; the long nursery pages have no in-page jump menu on the phone (the About, Gallery, Reviews, Fees, Contact tabs exist on desktop).

## Steal this
1. **Named rooms with a personality.** Fern, Thistle and Heather rooms turn a 0 to 5 age range into a journey. Use the nursery's own room names with age, number of places (only if the Care Inspectorate record or the nursery says so) and one sentence about what the room is like. No children needed: a corner of the room, the book shelf, the coat pegs without names.
2. **Name the cook and the dishes.** "Agata cooks Thai green curry, fish pie and chicken fajitas" is concrete and charming. Build a "Today's lunch" or "Meet our cook" block with the cook's first name (with consent) and last week's real menu. Use a photo of a plate or the kitchen, not a child.
3. **Settling-in as the lead story.** The two Airdrie quotes are both about patience at settling in. Put one parent quote (with written consent) next to a "How we settle your child in" box. Use hands holding a comforter, or a coat peg, as the image.
4. **Hand-painted brush-stroke dividers.** A real painted stripe, scanned from the nursery's own paint, replaces stock clip art. Ask the children to paint a long stroke on paper and scan it. This fits our rule that children's marks, not bought art, are the decoration.
5. **Per-nursery fee table on the page.** Publishing day, morning and afternoon prices beats "phone for fees". Present it with "Fees from [date]" and "phone to check" for anything missing, and add what the fee includes only when the nursery confirms.
6. **Tabbed nursery page (About, Gallery, Reviews, Fees, Contact).** On a phone, turn it into a sticky horizontal jump bar so a parent can go straight to Fees.
7. **"There is no such thing as bad weather" kit list.** A short "What to bring" with wellies and waterproofs as a friendly checklist. Illustrate with a drawn peg row, not photographed children.
8. **A short staff list with roles.** The "Meet our team" list shows names and roles. Add a small portrait per person (staff consent) to make it people-led.

## Avoid this
- A cookie banner that covers the hero on every first visit. Use a small bottom banner or none (self-host everything and avoid tracking so none is needed).
- Sliding or marquee reviews that clip text; the brief already forbids carousels.
- Stock photos as the hero when each nursery has real rooms and gardens.
- Unreferenced claims such as "mostly 5's and 6's" with no date or report link. Show grades per nursery with the report date and link, or nothing.
- Jargon-heavy paragraphs (attachment led practice, looking inwards, outwards and forwards) for anxious parents; write at reading age 9 to 11.
- A "news" page with out-of-date posts (pandemic return tips).
- 24 identical-looking small gallery photos of babies' faces; we can't reuse these.
