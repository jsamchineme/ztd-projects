## Why Kido, and what was substituted
The brief asked for three award-winning nurseries. The 2026 NDNA winners found by search were Young Friends (Brighton), Bright Beginnings (Leeds), Banbury Cross (Bridge of Don, Aberdeen; Nursery of the Year Scotland), Charnwood Nursery and Pre-school, and Puddle Ducks (South Wales) Ltd (Wales winner; not a typical nursery site, not studied). **Banbury Cross would have been ideal (Scottish and independent) but no working website could be found** after three web searches, a glow-blog fetch and URL guesses; it only appears in directories (daynurseries.co.uk lists it as a family-run 41-place nursery). **Charnwood** has several similarly named nurseries and no site could be confirmed. Kido is therefore a substitute: a well-known, premium London group (rated in daynurseries.co.uk's Top 20 groups three years running, by their claim). It is **less interesting** than hoped, and is included as the "premium, tech-forward chain" example.

## At a glance
- **Who:** an international group (UK, US, India, China sites) with a UK arm of London nurseries (Camden, Clerkenwell, Chiswick, Crouch End, Golders Green, Barnes, Battersea, Bethnal Green, Isleworth and more). "Since 2014, over 15,000 families across the world." Premium. Fees are not public on the pages we crawled; a calculator reveals them after choosing a nursery.
- **URL:** https://kidoschools.com/ (the old kido.school redirects here). UK section at /uk/.
- **Proof badges:** Ofsted Outstanding Provider badge, Daynurseries Top 20 Nursery Group 2025 badge, 9.6 daynurseries.co.uk score from 785 reviews.

## Site map
- **Home:** a country picker (UK, USA, India).
- **UK menu:** Our Nurseries, Funding, About (dropdown), Events, Blog, Careers, Contact. A side rail with email, Facebook and Instagram icons.
- **Page types:** UK home; nursery finder (map and list by borough); per-nursery tour pages with an embedded **YouCanBookMe calendar for each nursery**; Funding (guide download, 15 and 30 hour explainer, a calculator); Funding & Fees Calculator (a 5-step form); About ("for parents, by parents"); Curriculum & Programs (Kido STEM, Explorer Day, Languages, Sports & Dance, Cultural Events, Outings, Forest School); Safeguarding; Events (open days, webinars such as "Navigating Food Refusal"); Blog (parenting tips); Careers.

## How it sells
- **Promise:** "early years, reimagined"; "magical places where children play, learn, grow and thrive".
- **Tone:** aspirational and global. "Most advanced early years programs anywhere." Lots of method names (Montessori, Reggio Emilia, Multiple Intelligences, project-based).
- **Proof:** Ofsted badge, daynurseries Top 20 and score, a live review feed with dates, a Google rating per nursery card, an "owned by parents" origin story.
- **Differentiators:** in-house curriculum; STEM; weekly Spanish sessions (their claim); sport and dance specialists; Forest School; "connecting across the globe" (children video-call nurseries in other countries); a funding calculator; an events programme for parents.
- **Urgency:** open days (e.g. "Open Day at Wandsworth Bridge - 26th September 2026").

## Conversion mechanics
- **CTAs:** orange "Find a nursery near you" in the hero; "Book a Tour" on each page; "Download Funding Guide"; "Click Here to See How Much You Can Save".
- **Tour booking:** each nursery has an embedded **calendar booking widget** (YouCanBookMe), so a parent chooses a time slot themselves. This is the most self-serve flow of the five.
- **Fees calculator:** "Step 1 of 5, choose a nursery", then (later steps not inspected) funded hours and fees.
- **Funding:** the page explains the English 15 and 30 hour entitlements and says Kido accepts Tax-Free Childcare and Universal Credit. Funding is stretched over 51 weeks.
- **Phone / chat:** a contact page with an email; no phone number prominent on the UK page we saw.
- **Pop-ups:** a cookie consent panel.

## Visual design
- **Typefaces:** Google Fonts **Quicksand** (rounded geometric headings in a pale blue weight) and **Montserrat** (nav and buttons), seen in the page head.
- **Palette:** deep navy (#003366-ish, estimated from screenshots), a burnt-orange button (#c8581a-ish), white, a studio blue backdrop. School-uniform navy.
- **Illustration:** none beyond the logo.
- **Photography:** polished studio-style children in navy uniform T-shirts, shot against solid colours, faces forward (magnifying glass, lab beaker, balloons). Strong, but very much staged; and unusable for us.
- **Motion:** carousels for nurseries, curriculum cards and reviews. Mostly default Bootstrap-style.
- **Density:** medium. Long centred paragraphs are hard to read.
- **Fun vs premium:** the premium look comes from the uniform navy and the studio photos; fun is low. The orange button is the only warm accent.

## Phone experience
- Works: big orange button, nursery cards with grades and scores at a glance, calendar booking.
- Doesn't: black cookie box covers half of the first screen; long centred text blocks; three carousels; very small review text; footer is centre-stacked links with tiny targets.

## Steal this
1. **Self-serve tour calendar.** For a small nursery, a simple "choose a visit time" (the nursery supplies slots) is worth offering; where that is not possible, a form with "preferred days" and a phone number.
2. **Nursery card with Ofsted grade, daynurseries score and Google rating on one row.** For us: Care Inspectorate grades with the date, and review score only where the nursery supplies the source.
3. **"Funding & fees calculator" idea**, scaled down for Scotland: a short "How funded hours work for 3-5s and eligible 2s" explainer linked to the council's own page, no calculator (we cannot calculate reliably).
4. **Weekly specialist programmes as named cards** (Languages, Sports & Dance, Forest School). Only if the nursery really offers them, with day and frequency.
5. **"Owned by parents" origin story** as a human proof point. A small nursery can use its own real founder story (with its permission).
6. **An events strip** (open day, parent talk) as a soft conversion path.

## Avoid this
- Global country picker as the front door; long mission text for a single site.
- Staged studio photos with identifiable children.
- Too many method names (Montessori, Reggio, Multiple Intelligences) without saying what a child does all day.
- Centred long paragraphs; low-contrast pale-blue headings.
- Cookie consent that covers the hero.
