# Tops Day Nurseries (topsdaynurseries.co.uk)

Studied 13 October 2026. Crawl and screenshots: `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-samuelosuh-codes-mapboard/c179b3f0-507a-47ce-a937-2eef0da893bf/scratchpad/study-2/tops/` (plus `clean/tops-home-*.png`, with the cookie banner removed).

## At a glance
- **Who:** a South of England group, 33 nurseries (Dorset 11, Bournemouth & Poole 7, Hampshire 8, Portsmouth 5, Devon 6, Somerset 4, Wiltshire 3, Isle of Wight 1 on the finder) plus 12 holiday clubs for ages 5 to 11. Started in 1990 (a Parkstone nursery caring for four children), founded by Cheryl Hadland, who says she was inspired by seeing plastic pollution while diving. Employee-owned (an Employee Ownership Trust since April 2024). B Corp certified (footer badge).
- **Position:** "Exceptional care, practical value": value-priced and eco-led. No childcare fees are published. The only prices on the site are for the merchandise shop (for example hoodies at £21.54). One nursery is described as having a "subsidised rate".
- **Hub:** a hub site with each nursery listed under county pages, plus a parent portal "MyTops", online payments, a shop and Eventbrite events.
- **Not Scotland.** England only (South).

## How it sells
- **Promise:** "Growing minds for a greener future", "Caring for your child today means caring for their future." This is the most distinct purpose of the five: climate and a "green legacy".
- **Tone:** earnest, values-led, slightly corporate ("pioneering, purpose-driven community"). Warm in the parent quotes. Lots of long, jargon-heavy sentences ("Education for Sustainable Citizenship").
- **Proof:** award timeline (NDNA, NMT, local business awards), B Corp, King's Award, Top 100 Apprenticeship Employers, the founder's story, and parent quotes by first name with no date. There is no review score and no Ofsted grade on the pages we read (the FAQ has "Are you Ofsted registered?").
- **Differentiators:**
  - Sustainability: net zero and tree planting, B Corp, "passing the cost savings on to parents".
  - **Food:** "no added sugar" menus since 2016 (which "gained national attention"), a game menu with venison, vegan and vegetarian weekly, a chef, Pete, named and photographed as Nursery Chef of the Year, and an Instagram account for recipes. "Our Famous Menus" has its own page. A "recipe newsletter".
  - Employee ownership (consistent staff, familiar faces).
  - All-inclusive flexible packages (30 to 50 hours a week), extended hours (7am to 6pm), no term-time-only places (funding is stretched over the year).
  - Tops Cooking School and Tops Forest Skills, yoga and music.
  - Holiday clubs for ages 5 to 11, a shop with branded merchandise, a parent portal.
- **Urgency or scarcity:** none.

## Conversion mechanics
- **CTAs:** "Get in Touch" (a white button in the header), "View Our Locations" (the hero), "More Information" on each nursery card, "Arrange a visit" on each nursery page (per the Get in Touch page), "Book your Visit" in the FAQ. The phone and email icons are on the mobile header, so a parent can call in one tap.
- **Visit flow:** from the nursery page choose "Arrange a visit". The FAQ says "We'd love to welcome you for a personal tour... Schedule your nursery tour today." We did not see the nursery-level form; the global "Get in Touch" form has: name, telephone number, email, "Does your query relate to a nursery? (optional)", "How can we help?", "Where did you hear about us?" and a consent tick. Phone: 01202 551553. A separate recruitment line.
- **Fees transparency:** no nursery fees. The funding page explains 9-month, 2-year, 3-year and 4-year funding with the stretched model ("up to 52 weeks stretched at approximately 22 hours per week") and repeats "We do not offer term-time only places." (It is England-only; no Scottish funding.) It also has tax-free childcare (£2 for every £8) and a workplace benefit scheme. It says to talk to the nursery about pricing "in person".
- **Waiting list:** not mentioned.
- **Chat widgets or pop-ups:** a Cookiebot dialog that covers most of a phone screen. No chat.
- **Finder:** a search box and a radius selector (5, 10, 25, 50 miles) on the county pages.

## Visual design
- **Typefaces:** Montserrat 700 for headings (H1 56px on desktop), "Acumin Variable" (Adobe Fonts) for body, and a quirky handwriting-style face ("Grandstander") for "Parent Stories" and signatures. Two or three families, mixed well.
- **Palette:** a very deep **forest green `#053D3B`** (header, hero, footer, headings), a **mint `#EAF7F0`** and cream/peach tints for cards, a mid green **`#66CC99`**, **coral/orange** (`#E76F51`-ish) for the logo and sticker accents, sunflower yellow `#F4B942`-ish, and a lavender-purple `#67589A` that shows in the cookie dialog. A natural, earthy palette with a strong dark base.
- **Illustration:** a **sticker set**: friendly flat animals and insects (bee, butterfly, whale, starfish, hedgehog, ladybird, chick, sun) with a white outline, positioned on corners and flight paths (dashed lines). Plus line doodles (bee, butterfly, chick, cloud) as a divider. The set is consistent and fresh, and not primary-coloured.
- **Photography:** a mix: studio-style, bright "stock-like" portraits of laughing children in rounded colour tiles in the hero, and real documentary photos taken on phones of the nurseries (children in hi-vis outdoors, a child watering plants, an adult with a baby, seen from behind). The hero portraits feel like stock. The nursery cards feel real and honest, though uneven in quality.
- **Shapes and textures:** large rounded panels (about 32px), tilted cards with dashed borders, taped polaroid cards, rounded photo tiles, pill buttons in white on green. Slight rotation (1 to 3 degrees) gives a scrapbook feel.
- **Motion:** sliders for testimonials, filters on awards. We captured no other motion.
- **Density:** medium. Card-led, with centred short paragraphs. The inner pages are text-heavy.
- **Fun and engaging:** the stickers and tilted dashed cards; the polaroid awards wall; the cooking and forest-skills activities.
- **Premium:** the deep green and cream, the Montserrat headings.
- **Twee, cheap or generic:** hero portraits that look stock-bought; centred small body text; wordy values copy; a shop selling hoodies feels off-message for a parent audience.

## Phone experience
- **Works:** one tap to call or email from the header; the hero is a single clear promise with two big buttons; sticker cards break up the page; the county list; footer links as simple lists, not accordions (easy to scan).
- **Doesn't:** the cookie dialog covers the first screen; very long page (12,000px) with centred body text; tilted cards make text a little harder to read (rotated text on a phone can blur); the testimonial slider has no dates or sources; the alignment of the awards wall is a long scroll of repeated polaroids; the nursery cards show a truncated "Welcome to Tops Day Nurseries: Wimborne Nursery, Dorset Here at..." with no useful fact (no hours, ages or places).

## Steal this
1. **A sticker set of small animals and insects, as the nursery's own decoration.** A bee, a hedgehog, a ladybird, a chick, with white outlines and in the nursery's colours. For Scotland: a puffin, a red squirrel, a highland cow, a thistle, a heron, a seal, a midge's-eye view of a loch. Draw them flat, so they cost nothing to load. They put personality on the page with no identifiable children.
2. **Tilted dashed-border cards for "what makes us different".** Each card gets one sticker, one heading and two lines. Keep tilt small (1 to 2 degrees) and check the text is still crisp at 390px.
3. **A polaroid-and-tape wall for awards and "things we've done".** For a small nursery: "Our latest Care Inspectorate report", "Healthy Living Award", "Our local gardening show rosette", with the real date. Only list things the nursery confirms.
4. **One big purpose.** Tops has climate, Kids Planet has outdoors, Busy Bees has science. A small Scottish nursery can own one thing that's true: the garden, the kitchen, the Gaelic songs, the wee woodland walk, the forest kindergarten. Build the whole homepage around that one sentence.
5. **Make the food a headline act.** A page called "Our famous menus" with the chef named and a recipe newsletter turns lunch into a reason to choose the nursery. For us: this week's menu as a printed-looking card, the cook's first name, a "make it at home" recipe. Only claim "no added sugar" or an award if the nursery can prove it.
6. **Phone and email icons in the mobile header.** A one-tap call is the best conversion mechanic on a phone.
7. **Rounded colour tiles behind photos.** In the hero, photos sit on green, orange and yellow rounded squares. We can do the same with colour tiles holding the garden gate, a coat peg, a painted stone, or a child's drawing.
8. **A handwriting font for quotes only.** One handwritten face (self-hosted) for "Parent stories" and signatures, and a plain sans for everything else. Check that it stays legible.
9. **Stretched funding explained in one sentence per age group.** "Up to 52 weeks stretched at approximately 22 hours a week." Adapt to Scotland with the nursery's confirmed model.
10. **A radius selector on the finder** is not needed for one nursery, but an "area we serve" list is (the villages or postcodes it draws from).

## Avoid this
- Laughing-child portraits that look like stock. We can't reuse real faces; anything that reads as stock breaks our own rule.
- A hero with three portrait tiles and no sign of the actual nursery or building.
- Parent quotes with a first name only, no date and no source.
- No Ofsted or inspection grade on the homepage. We will show the Care Inspectorate grades with the report date.
- Copy that sounds like a corporate report ("pioneering, purpose-driven community... green legacies"). Parents want short plain sentences.
- A merchandise shop in the main nav, and no fees at all.
- Cookie dialogs.
- Rotated body text on small screens.
- Venison and game on a toddler menu as a selling point will not suit every Scottish parent. Use food as a headline only where it is true and welcome.
