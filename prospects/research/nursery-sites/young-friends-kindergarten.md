## How it sells
- **Promise:** one big idea per page. "A sustainable, plant-based kindergarten", "children grow, explore and thrive, naturally". The values are the product.
- **Tone:** warm, confident, slightly earnest. Short sentences, repeated frames ("calm, social and unhurried", "children's engagement and flow"). Nothing is jokey, but it is vivid.
- **Real phrases:** "Nothing here is tokenistic or staged." "Instead of conventional toys, children explore with open-ended materials: wood, fabric, metal and natural objects." "a setting that hasn't been tidied up or staged for your arrival." "Food is never rushed, pressured or used as reward." "going to her second family three days a week" (parent quote).
- **Proof:** NDNA double win with the judges' own words quoted at length on a dedicated page; Ofsted Outstanding with a link; parent quotes labelled by room and date (room names: Hedgehog, Fox); staff with qualification levels listed (Level 6 / Level 3); a Trustpilot profile exists but is not linked from the pages we saw.
- **Differentiators:** fully plant-based on-site kitchen with children cooking; kitchen garden; loose parts; "schemas" (plain-English explainer posts); a colour-coded emotional regulation system ("The Just Right System": green, amber, blue, red); washable nappies; composting; settling-in sessions included in fees.
- **Urgency / scarcity:** none shown. The scarcity is implied by the awards and by minimum two sessions a week and by "visits are short and low-key".

## Conversion mechanics
- **CTAs:** "Book a visit" appears in the header (twice on desktop: header pill and in the hero), on every inner page hero, mid-page and in the footer. It links to a Famly enquiry form on app.famly.co (the third-party parent-app vendor), not an on-site form.
- **Visit flow (from the Visit page):** Step 1 fill in the enquiry form (details about child and needs); Step 2 they contact you to arrange a visit and send an information video beforehand; Step 3 visit (short, low-key, staff stay focused on children, you jot questions down) then time to ask questions in a calm space. Contact page repeats the three steps. Fields in the Famly form were not inspected (external).
- **Phone:** 01273 735100 on the Contact page.
- **Fees transparency:** high. A dedicated Fees page with a table, hourly rate, optional extras when using funded hours (meals £16 a day, consumables £1.20 an hour), and two named routes ("Full Young Friends Experience" or "Funded-Only Care", the latter in limited blocks). A separate "Early Years Funding Explained" post.
- **Waiting list:** not mentioned. **Chat widget / pop-up:** none seen.
- **Weakness:** the visit CTA goes out to a different domain with a long query string; the funding page has two links with a malformed "https://https//" prefix.

## Visual design
- **Typefaces (from page CSS):** Google Fonts **Chelsea Market** (a chunky, friendly display face) for headings and buttons; **Mulish** for body. Inter and Montserrat are also loaded. (Self-hosting needed for our builds.)
- **Palette (hex counts in the CSS):** sage green #7daa83, dusty teal #7da6a8, mustard #c1a83e, warm paper #f5f1e9, terracotta/orange #dda15f and #c65c2e, brick red #924437, forest green (card #3a5a45-ish from screenshots), light sage #abbcad. Very earthy and 70s-natural, not primary colours.
- **Illustration:** loose hand-drawn doodles (spirals, fern fronds, dot clusters, leaf shapes, a mushroom-like blob) in brand colours, overlapping card corners. A leaf logo.
- **Shapes:** the signature is a **"leaf" radius**: boxes and buttons have one or two big rounded corners and the others nearly square. Applied to buttons, cards and photo frames (arched photo frames).
- **Texture:** the page background is a speckled recycled-paper texture, not flat white.
- **Photography:** real, candid, mostly children from behind or hands in soil, hats, wellies, a toddler on a bean frame. Almost no posed faces on the home page. Warm, natural light. This is the most consent-friendly approach of the five.
- **Iconography:** small bespoke SVG pictograms and doodles, no stock icon set.
- **Motion:** images fade or reveal on scroll (our screenshot caught several photo frames empty until the lazy image loaded), a testimonial carousel. No auto-play video.
- **Density:** generous space; a lot of vertical white space on desktop.
- **Fun vs premium:** fun from the rounded display type, colour cards and doodles. Premium from the restraint, paper texture, long-form values copy and awards. Nothing felt twee. The risk is that colour contrast on the mustard and sage buttons with white text is weak (white on #c1a83e is low contrast) and light-weight body text on paper.

## Phone experience
- Works: single column, large tappable pill buttons at the top, strong hero, cards stack cleanly with room between them, readable body at about 16px.
- Doesn't: two buttons plus a hamburger bar eat about 170px before the hero; hero text overlaps a busy photo (white text on soil and hands, readable only thanks to a shadow); some lines in Chelsea Market wrap tightly with tight leading; long copy pages (Approach is about 1,200 words).

## Steal this
1. **One conviction, shown everywhere.** Pick what the nursery really does (e.g. a named woodland, a baking kitchen, a croft-style garden) and make the home page H1 and sub say it. Use only facts the nursery gives us.
2. **Colour-coded card stack as the home-page menu.** Six large cards in six earthy colours, one sentence each, one button. Works without any photo of children.
3. **Leaf / one-corner radius as a brand shape.** A single CSS rule (large radius on two opposite corners) used on buttons, cards and photo frames makes any layout feel designed. Easy to adapt to a Scottish motif (thistle-leaf or hill shape).
4. **Hand-drawn doodle overlays** at card corners; we can commission or draw simple shapes per nursery and lock them in the template as SVG.
5. **Paper-texture background** (a 20 kB tiled noise image or CSS) instead of flat white for warmth.
6. **"How visits work" as a three-step explainer** with a note about what a visit is like, a short prep video or photo walk-through, and an honest line about who it suits ("Is this right for you?"). Reduces anxious parents' fear of a sales pitch. We can use a room photo tour instead of a video.
7. **Photographs of hands, soil, wellies, children from behind**, plus children's own artwork frames. Maps directly onto our no-identifiable-children rule.
8. **Judges' quotes and parent quotes on their own pages** with the person and date labelled by room, not by name. We can only do this where the nursery supplies real quotes.
9. **Fee page with a "what's included" icon grid** (meals, activities, care) that makes the price feel earned. Needs real numbers from the nursery; otherwise "phone to check".
10. **Plain-English explainers for jargon** ("What are schemas?", "What are loose parts?") as short, linked pages. Good SEO and trust building.

## Avoid this
- White text on mustard or sage buttons (fails WCAG AA contrast). Use dark text or deeper tones.
- Parent CTA that leaves the domain to a third-party form with no fallback phone number nearby.
- Lazy-loaded photo frames showing empty outlines on slow connections.
- Google Fonts and a third-party CDN: we must self-host.
- Claiming "Outstanding" unless the nursery's current grade says so. In Scotland use Care Inspectorate grades and dates.
